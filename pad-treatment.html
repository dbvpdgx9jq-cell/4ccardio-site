Leg pain & circulation · Miami & Doral

Leg pain when walking? It could be your circulation.

Your legs are trying to tell you something.

Do your calves, thighs or hips cramp, burn or ache when you walk, then ease up when you stop? That's called claudication, one of the most common signs of blocked leg arteries, known as peripheral artery disease (PAD). It isn't just getting older, and it can be treated.

Questions patients ask

What is claudication?

Claudication is cramping, burning or aching in the calf, thigh or hip that comes on with walking and eases when you stop. It happens because a narrowed artery can't deliver enough blood to working muscles. It is the most common symptom of peripheral artery disease.

Is leg pain when walking just a sign of age?

No. Many people put it down to arthritis or getting older, but pain that reliably starts with walking and stops with rest often means blocked leg arteries. A painless test in our office can tell the difference.

What is PAD (peripheral artery disease)?

PAD is a narrowing or blockage of the arteries that carry blood to the legs, caused by the same plaque that causes heart attacks. It reduces blood flow to the muscles and skin of the leg and foot.

How do I know if I have it?

A painless test in our office, the ankle-brachial index (ABI), compares blood pressure in your ankles and arms. An arterial ultrasound then shows where any blockage is. Both take less than an hour.

Does a blockage always need a procedure?

No. Many patients with claudication improve with a structured walking program and medication. A procedure is considered when symptoms limit your daily life despite treatment, or when a wound or rest pain means the leg is at risk.

Is this open surgery?

No. When a procedure is needed, it is endovascular, a minimally invasive treatment performed through a small puncture in an artery, usually in the groin, with local anesthesia and light sedation. There is no surgical incision, but the puncture site still needs care while it heals, and we go over those instructions with you. Most patients go home the same day.

How long is recovery?

Most people walk the same evening and return to normal activity within a few days. Your doctor will tell you about any limits for the puncture site.

What's the difference between poor circulation and varicose veins?

Poor arterial circulation means not enough blood is reaching the leg, which causes pain with walking, wounds and cold feet. Varicose veins are a problem with blood returning from the leg, which causes aching, heaviness and swelling. We evaluate and treat both. See vein treatment.

Do I need a referral?

It depends on your insurance plan. Call us with your insurance card in hand and we will tell you, and we verify your coverage and copay before your appointment.

Is PAD treatment covered by insurance?

Evaluation and treatment of PAD are covered by Medicare and most plans when medically necessary. We verify your coverage and copay before your appointment.
