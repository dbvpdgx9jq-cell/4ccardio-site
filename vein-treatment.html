Varicose veins & venous insufficiency · Miami & Doral

Heavy, aching legs and varicose veins, treated in the office.

When the valves in the leg veins stop working, blood pools in the legs. The result is aching, heaviness, swelling, night cramps, bulging veins and, over time, darkened skin and wounds near the ankle. It is a medical condition, not only a cosmetic one, and it can be treated in the office with local anesthesia.

Signs of venous insufficiency

Heavy, tired legs

Aching, heaviness or throbbing that gets worse through the day or after standing, and feels better when you put your feet up.

Swelling and cramps

Ankles that swell by evening, leg cramps or restless legs at night, and itching over the veins.

Skin changes and wounds

Brown or darkened skin around the ankle, thickened skin, or a sore near the ankle that is slow to heal. These need treatment, not just stockings.

How it works

From ultrasound to treatment

A standing venous ultrasound shows which veins have failed valves. Treatment closes those veins so blood reroutes through healthy ones.

Call to schedule
  1. Office visit with a venous ultrasound to measure reflux in each vein.
  2. If your plan requires it, a period of compression stockings first. We tell you exactly what your insurance asks for and document it.
  3. Radiofrequency ablation seals the failing vein from the inside with heat, using only local numbing medicine. Varithena foam or sclerotherapy treats twisted or smaller veins.
  4. You walk out afterward and return to most activities within a day or two, with a follow-up ultrasound to confirm the vein is closed.

Questions patients ask

Are varicose veins just cosmetic?

No. Varicose veins that cause aching, swelling, cramps or skin changes are a sign of venous insufficiency, a medical condition that can progress to skin damage and ulcers if untreated.

Does the procedure hurt?

Radiofrequency ablation is done with local numbing medicine. Most people feel a few needle sticks and pressure. You are awake, and you walk out afterward.

How long is recovery?

Most people return to work and normal activity within a day or two. Walking is encouraged right away. Heavy exercise is usually limited for about a week.

Is vein treatment covered by insurance?

Usually, when the ultrasound shows reflux and you have symptoms. Many plans require a trial of compression stockings first. We verify your coverage and handle the documentation your plan requires.

What about spider veins?

Spider veins on their own are usually considered cosmetic and are not covered by insurance. If you have both, we treat the underlying vein problem first.

Will the veins come back?

A treated vein stays closed in the large majority of patients. New veins can develop over time, which is why we follow you with ultrasound.

Where are treatments done?

In our offices, with local anesthesia. Call 305.856.1064 and we will book the ultrasound and visit at the location most convenient for you.
