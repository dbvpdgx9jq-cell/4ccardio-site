Diabetic foot wounds & limb preservation

A wound that won't heal may be a blood flow problem.

Some foot wounds, particularly in people with diabetes, struggle to heal because of reduced blood flow. When poor circulation is contributing to a wound that won't heal, improving blood flow may be an important part of healing and limb preservation.

What we do

Restore blood flow, protect the limb.

Dr. Marcos Valerio is board certified in interventional cardiology and treats blocked leg arteries in our own office-based lab in Doral.

  1. Prompt circulation testing.Ankle pressures and arterial ultrasound in our office. Ankle pressures can read falsely normal in people with diabetes or kidney disease, so additional testing is used when needed.
  2. Advanced vascular imaging.When needed, detailed imaging of the arteries with an angiogram and ultrasound from inside the artery (IVUS) to find the blockage and guide treatment.

Emergency

Call 911 if a foot or leg suddenly turns cold, pale, numb and very painful. That needs the emergency room, not an office appointment.

Questions patients ask

Why won't my foot wound heal?

Wounds need blood flow to heal. When the arteries to the foot are narrowed or blocked, even excellent wound care may not be enough. Checking circulation is one of the first steps when a foot wound is slow to heal, especially with diabetes.

I have diabetes. Am I at higher risk?

Yes. People with diabetes have a higher risk of peripheral artery disease, and it often affects the smaller arteries below the knee. Diabetes can also reduce feeling in the feet, so a wound may go unnoticed.

An amputation has been mentioned. What should I do?

Ask whether your circulation has been fully evaluated. Anyone facing a major amputation should have their blood flow checked to see whether it can be restored. Call us and we will see you promptly.

Will I stop seeing my podiatrist?

No. Your podiatrist and wound care team keep caring for the wound. We focus on the circulation and send them our findings and plan.

What does treatment involve?

It starts with testing in our office. When an artery needs to be opened, it is done through a small puncture in our office-based lab in Doral, and most patients go home the same day.
